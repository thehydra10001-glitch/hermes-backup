# Amateur Radio Q-Codes Reference

Compiled from Indian ASOC/GIAR exam study materials. Q-codes are three-letter codes standardized by the ITU for radiocommunications.

## Signal Quality & Strength

| Code | Question | Answer |
|:-----|:---------|:-------|
| QSA | What is the strength of my signal? | 1=bad, 2=poor, 3=fair, 4=good, 5=excellent |
| QRK | What is the intelligibility of my signal? | 1=bad, 2=poor, 3=fair, 4=good, 5=excellent |
| QSB | Are my signals fading? | Your signals are fading |
| QRM | Are you being interfered with? | nil/slightly/moderately/severely/extremely |
| QRN | Are you troubled by static? | nil/slightly/moderately/severely/extremely |

## Frequency & Timing

| Code | Question | Answer |
|:-----|:---------|:-------|
| QRG | Will you tell me my exact frequency? | Your frequency is ... kHz/MHz |
| QRX | Will you call me again? | I will call at ... hours (on ... kHz/MHz) |
| QSY | Shall I change frequency? | Change to transmission on another frequency |
| QTR | What is the correct time? | The time is ... hours |

## Station Identity & Communication

| Code | Question | Answer |
|:-----|:---------|:-------|
| QRA | What is the name of your station? | The name of my station is ... |
| QRL | Are you busy? | I am busy |
| QRV | Are you ready? | I am ready |
| QRZ | Who is calling me? | You are being called by ... |
| QSO | Can you communicate with ...? | I can communicate directly (or by relay through ...) |
| QSL | Can you acknowledge receipt? | I can acknowledge receipt |
| QTH | What is your position? | My position is ... latitude and ... longitude |

## Transmission Control

| Code | Question | Answer |
|:-----|:---------|:-------|
| QRO | Shall I increase power? | Increase your transmitter power |
| QRP | Shall I decrease power? | Decrease your transmitter power |
| QRQ | Shall I send faster? | Send faster (... wpm) |
| QRS | Shall I send more slowly? | Send more slowly (... wpm) |
| QRT | Shall I stop sending? | Stop sending |
| QRV | Are you ready? | I am ready |

## Frequently Examined Q-Codes (ASOC/GIAR focus)

| Code | Meaning | Notes |
|:-----|:--------|:------|
| QTR | What is the correct time? | Direct question for "time" |
| QSB | Are my signals fading? | S = Signals, B = Bad/fading |
| QRQ | Shall I send faster? | R = Rate, Q = quick |
| QRA | What is your station name? | A = Appellation/name |
| QRV | Are you ready? | V = Ready/available |
| QSY | Change to another frequency | SY = Shift frequency |
| QSO | Communication link | Most common general Q-code |
| QRM | Man-made interference | M = Man-made |
| QRN | Atmospheric/static interference | N = Natural/static |
| QRL | Are you busy? / The frequency is busy | L = Line busy |
| QRT | Stop sending | T = Terminate |
| QRX | I will call you again at ... | X = Later/callback |
| QRZ | You are being called by ... | Z = Who's calling |
| QTH | My position is ... | TH = There/position |

## Exam Tips

- **QTR** is the most commonly tested Q-code — "What is the correct time?"
- **QSB** = fading signals (the 'B' hints at bad/breaking up)
- **QRQ** = send faster, **QRS** = send slower (mnemonic: Q=Query, R=Rate, Q=Quick, S=Slow)
- **QRM** and **QRN** are both interference — QRM is man-made, QRN is natural/static
- **QRA** is one of the few Q-codes a new station MUST know (station identification)
- **QRV** (ready) vs **QRL** (busy) — opposite states, easy to confuse
